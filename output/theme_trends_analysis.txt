=========================================================

KEY TRENDS
----------

1. THE DECLINE OF CODE TALKS (2010-2019)
   Programming & Tools was the dominant theme from 2010-2015, peaking at
   7 out of 10 talks in 2011. By 2017, it had dropped to zero. The early
   years were about teaching the newsroom how to code; once the baseline
   was established, the conversation moved on.

2. THE RISE OF NEWSROOM CULTURE (2018-2019)
   Newsroom Culture & Career exploded in 2018 (5 talks) and 2019 (6 talks),
   making it the single biggest theme in those years. Talks covered unions,
   impostor syndrome, racism in code, alcohol in journalism, and career
   sustainability. This reflects a broader industry reckoning about
   workplace conditions and diversity.

3. DATA VIZ PEAKED EARLY, THEN FADED
   Data Visualization had its strongest years in 2010, 2014, and 2015
   (3-4 talks each). After 2016, it nearly disappeared from the lineup.
   Possible explanation: once tools like Datawrapper and Flourish became
   mainstream, the need to evangelize visualization techniques lessened.

4. STORYTIME IS THE LATE-ERA STAPLE
   Storytime (history, personal narratives, storytelling about data) barely
   existed before 2016 but became the top theme in 2020 (4 talks) and 2025
   (4 talks). Data journalists increasingly use lightning talks to share
   personal stories and lessons rather than technical how-tos.

5. FOIA SURGES IN CYCLES
   FOIA & Public Records shows a cyclical pattern: absent in the early years,
   it peaked in 2017 (2), 2019 (2), 2021 (3), and 2025 (3). These surges
   correlate with political administrations that were perceived as less
   transparent, suggesting FOIA advocacy intensifies in response to secrecy.

6. AI IS A SLOW BURN
   Despite the AI hype cycle, AI & Machine Learning has never had more than
   2 talks in a single year (2013, 2014). It appeared sporadically in
   2022-2024 with 1 talk each. Data journalists have been cautious adopters,
   not early evangelists.

7. DATA HABITS: THE EVERGREEN
   Data Habits (productivity, workflow, self-improvement) is the most
   consistent theme, appearing in 11 of 16 years. It peaked in 2013, 2017,
   and 2023 with 3 talks each. Journalists never stop wanting to work
   smarter.

8. HUMOR FADES BUT NEVER DIES
   Humor & Satire was most prominent in 2012-2013 (2-1 talks), had
   scattered appearances since, and remains a beloved tradition. The
   conference has gotten more serious, but the spirit of fun persists.

ERA SUMMARY
-----------
2010-2012: The Technical Era
  Dominated by Programming & Tools (17 of 30 talks). The community was
  building foundational coding skills.

2013-2016: The Diversification Era
  Themes broadened significantly. Data Viz, Data Habits, Elections, and
  AI all entered the mix. No single theme dominated.

2017-2019: The Culture Shift
  Newsroom Culture exploded. FOIA and Elections appeared as political
  awareness grew. Technical talks nearly vanished.

2020-2025: The Reflection Era
  Storytime and FOIA dominate. Journalists tell personal stories and
  fight for transparency. Investigations and accountability grow. The
  community has matured from "how to code" to "why we do this work."
